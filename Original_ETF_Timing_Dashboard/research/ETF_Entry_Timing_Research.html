Best Entry Windows for US Broad-Market ETFs

A rule-based, quantitative study identifying 3–5 high-conviction cash-deployment signals per year into VOO / QQQ / SCHG / VUG / IWY — evaluated across seasonality, drawdowns, volatility regimes, trend, macro filters and sentiment extremes.
Sample: 2000-01-03 → 2026-11-13 (26.9 years, 6,758 trading days) Instruments: SPY (primary), QQQ (validation) Method: event study + bootstrap, overlap-deduplicated
Bottom line — the three signals that actually beat "just buy anytime"

The single most important finding is a base-rate trap: over this sample, buying SPY on any random day delivered a positive 6-month return 72.6% of the time (average +4.78%). Many popular "signals" (e.g. golden cross 92% win rate) look impressive in isolation but are statistically indistinguishable from this baseline once sample size and bootstrap uncertainty are accounted for.

Only a small set of signals produced statistically significant excess returns (bootstrap p<0.05) with meaningful magnitude. These are the high-conviction entries:

  1. VIX crosses above 35 (panic entry) — +7.86pp excess 6m return, p=0.0006, 88.9% win rate, ~0.7 signals/yr.
  2. First 5% pullback from a 52-week high — +3.78pp excess, p=0.0094, 83.7% win rate, ~1.6 signals/yr. Best frequency × robustness trade-off.
  3. ≥5% pullback while price holds above the 200-day MA — +3.75pp excess, p=0.013, 83.8% win rate, ~1.4 signals/yr.

Counter-intuitive result: deeper corrections (10%, 15%, 20%) were worse entry points than a shallow 5% pullback. Buying the first dip in an intact uptrend beat catching the falling knife — deeper declines concentrated in 2000–02 and 2008 carried persistent macro damage.

1. Methodology & Data

Return measurement

Entry at the close of day t on which a signal fires; forward returns measured to t+63 (3m), t+126 (6m) and t+252 (12m) trading days using dividend-adjusted prices. "Maximum drawdown after entry" is the average worst peak-to-trough decline experienced over the 12 months following entry.

Overlap de-duplication

Consecutive signals within a 21–63 day window are clustered into a single episode (entry at first trigger). This prevents a single market event from being counted as dozens of independent observations and inflating significance — a common flaw in seasonality studies.

Statistical tests

Each signal's mean forward return is compared to the unconditional baseline via a 5,000-draw bootstrap (sampling baseline returns of equal size). Reported p-value = probability the true excess is ≤ 0. Sub-period stability splits the sample at 2013 (early vs. late). QQQ is used as an out-of-sample instrument check.

Data sources & limitations

  • Prices/volatility: Yahoo Finance chart API (SPY, QQQ, ^VIX, TLT, ^IRX 13-week bill, ^TNX 10-year) — 1999–2026.
  • Macro proxies: yield-curve slope proxied by 10Y minus 13-week bill (^TNX−^IRX); disinflation proxied by TLT trending above its 6-month average. Direct CPI / ISM / Fed-funds-target history (FRED) was inaccessible, so "Fed cutting" and "ISM recovering" filters are proxied, not exact and should be treated as lower-confidence.
  • Sentiment: AAII, CNN Fear & Greed and CBOE put/call historical APIs were inaccessible. VIX percentile (3-year rolling) is used as the fear proxy. Sentiment findings are therefore indicative only.
  • Presidential-cycle effects were tested but produced <8 independent episodes over 27 years — too few for reliable inference, and are excluded from conclusions.
  • Results are gross of commissions/slippage (negligible for monthly ETF entries) and use adjusted closes which embed dividends. No look-ahead: signals use only data up to and including day t.

2. Full Strategy Ranking

How to read: "Win 6m" is the raw win rate — always compare it to the 72.6% baseline. "Excess" is the bootstrap-adjusted outperformance vs. baseline; only values with p<0.05 are statistically reliable. p<.05 = significant.

3. Tier A — Top 3 High-Conviction Signals

TIER A · Deploy cash on these three rule-based triggers

#1 · Highest convictionVIX crosses above 35 — capitulation entry

Rule: Buy on the first day ^VIX closes above 35 after being at/below 35 the prior session. Split cash into 2 tranches if VIX exceeds 40.

Evidence: 18 episodes (~0.67/yr), 6m win 88.9% (+16.2pp vs baseline), avg 6m +12.64% (excess +7.86pp, p=0.0006), avg 12m +21.46%, avg post-entry max DD −8.34%. QQQ confirmed: +18.03% avg 6m. Stable across sub-periods (early +7.9%, late +16.4%).

Catch: Rare — fires roughly 2 years in 3. 2008, 2020 and 2025 each produced 2–3 clustered signals. Patient cash is required.

#2 · Best frequency × robustnessFirst 5% pullback from a 52-week high

Rule: Buy when SPY first closes ≥5% below its trailing 252-day high, after being above −5% the prior day. Do not wait for 10%.

Evidence: 43 episodes (~1.6/yr), 6m win 83.7% (+11.1pp), avg 6m +8.56% (excess +3.78pp, p=0.0094), avg 12m +13.57%, avg post-entry max DD −7.82% (best risk profile of all signals). QQQ +10.88%. Sub-periods: early +6.5%, late +10.0%.

Why not deeper: 10% pullbacks yielded only +4.51% avg 6m (63.6% win), 15% +4.71% (47.4% win), 20% −1.36% (46.2% win). The "buy more dip" intuition is not supported.

#3 · Trend-confirmed≥5% pullback while above the 200-day MA

Rule: Buy on a day when SPY is ≥5% below its 52-week high and still above its 200-day moving average (first occurrence of the condition).

Evidence: 37 episodes (~1.4/yr), 6m win 83.8% (+11.1pp), avg 6m +8.53% (excess +3.75pp, p=0.013), avg 12m +12.98%, avg post-entry max DD −8.53%. QQQ +11.04%. Early +5.7%, late +10.5%.

Use case: The most "implementable" signal — it naturally keeps you out of structural bear markets while buying healthy dips.

On the golden cross: it shows the highest raw win rate (92.3%) and an attractive −6.4% post-entry drawdown, which is why it ranks near the top on naive screens. But with only 13 episodes the bootstrap excess is not significant (p=0.20, CI −3.3 to +9.0pp). Treat it as a confirmation filter, not a standalone high-conviction trigger.

4. Tier B — Best Combination Strategy

TIER B · Multi-condition combination (use to upgrade conviction, not to create more trades)

Combo F: VIX > 30 AND drawdown ≤ 10% AND yield curve positive (10Y > 13W)

Evidence: 24 episodes (~0.9/yr), avg 6m +8.39% (excess +3.62pp, p=0.049 — borderline), avg 12m +17.18%, QQQ avg 6m +14.11% with 83.3% win. Stable across sub-periods (early +7.3%, late +14.0%).

QQQ advantage: the combination's edge is materially stronger on the Nasdaq-100 (+14.11% vs +8.39% on SPY) — growth ETFs (QQQ/SCHG/VUG/IWY) respond more violently to panic-and-recovery setups.

Runner-up: Combo A — VIX > 30 AND drawdown ≤ 10% (no curve filter)

25 episodes, avg 6m +7.61% (excess +2.83pp, p=0.106 — not significant), avg 12m +15.79%. The curve filter in Combo F is what lifts it over the significance threshold.

Does combining signals materially raise win probability? No — not on this data. Combo F's raw 6m win rate (70.8%) is actually below the 72.6% baseline. What combinations do is raise mean return and 12-month magnitude (fat right tail) at the cost of lower signal frequency and slightly deeper interim drawdowns. For maximizing the probability of any positive 6-month outcome, Tier A's shallow-dip signals are better.

5. Signal Families — What Worked and What Didn't

Seasonality

Weak but mildly useful. Looking at all entry days within each month (chart), the strongest 6m forward returns were October (+5.87%), March (+5.94%) and November (+5.21%); the weakest were April (+3.75%) and May (+4.09%). Restricting to early-month entries, September stood out (+5.19% avg, 81.8% win) and December (+5.06%, 80.8% win), but neither beat the baseline with statistical significance. The classic "Sell in May" (buy early May) underperformed (+3.18% avg, unstable); the Halloween effect (buy early November) gave +6.02% but only 69.2% win. Use seasonality as a tie-breaker, not a primary signal.

Drawdown

Non-monotonic. Shallow 5% dip = strong; 10%/15%/20% progressively weaker and unstable. Conclusion: the market's first dip in an uptrend is a high-probability event; deep bear-market dips carry regime risk that overwhelms the valuation appeal. See the excess chart.

Volatility / VIX

The strongest family. VIX>35 is the single best signal. VIX>30 is good (+6.83% avg 6m, 64.5% win) but not significant. VIX>25 alone is poor (2.82%, 60% win). "VIX spike then 20% decline" gave +4.9% — the panic must be extreme, not moderate. Fear extremes (VIX top-10% of 3y range) gave +6.04% but unstable across sub-periods.

7. Monthly Decision Framework (Automatable)

  1. Run on the last trading day of each month (or the 1st). No daily trading required.
  2. Compute the 5 inputs: SPY vs 200DMA, drawdown from 252-day high, ^VIX level, TLT vs 6m MA, curve (10Y−13W), plus calendar month.
  3. Check triggers in priority order:
    • If VIX > 35 (cross) → STRONG BUY deploy 40–50% of designated cash immediately, 2 tranches if VIX>40.
    • Else if DD ≥ 5% and above 200DMA (first cross) → BUY deploy 25–30%.
    • Else if VIX > 30 and DD ≥ 10% and curve > 0 → BUY deploy 25–30% (favor QQQ/SCHG/VUG).
    • Else use Score: ≥75 Strong Buy, 61–74 Buy, 49–60 hold/neutral, ≤48 WAIT (keep cash in T-bills).
  4. Seasonal tie-break: in September, October, November and December, allow a Buy at score ≥61 rather than ≥75. In May, require ≥75.
  5. Exhaustion rule: after any Strong Buy, pause new entries for 30 trading days to avoid clustering multiple tranches into one event.
  6. Instrument choice: for panic entries (VIX>30/35) lean toward QQQ / SCHG / VUG / IWY (higher beta to recovery); for shallow-dip/200DMA entries, VOO / SPY are sufficient.

9. Key Caveats

  • Sample includes the 2000s "lost decade" and the 2008/2020 crashes — deep-dip underperformance may partly reflect those specific macro regimes and could differ in a low-rate secular bull environment.
  • Fewer than 25 independent episodes for most signals — confidence intervals are wide; the ranking is more reliable than any single point estimate.
  • Multiple comparisons were tested (~45); the three Tier A signals survived bootstrap significance, but some degree of selection bias cannot be excluded. VIX>35 has the strongest claim (p=0.0006).
  • Macro and sentiment filters use proxies; conclusions there are lower-confidence than price/volatility signals.
  • This is a research framework for staged cash deployment, not a timing oracle. No strategy here avoids drawdowns — even the best signal averaged −8% interim drawdown after entry.
