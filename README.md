> **v3 — multi-target, regime-aware, dual schedule (2026-11-19).**
> `index_v3.html` is a third, separate build; v1 and v2 are left byte-for-byte
> untouched. `pipeline/evidence_engine_v3.py` **imports v2 and reuses its
> anti-lookahead primitives unchanged** rather than rewriting them:
> - **Phase 1** targets: A (`> PIT historical median`), B (`>10%`), C (`>15%`),
>   D (`> T-bill + 5%`, primary). Target A's median uses a canonical month-end
>   grid of *already-realised* outcomes, so it is point-in-time and
>   schedule-independent. 4 targets × 4 horizons × 2 tickers × 2 schedules =
>   **64 full model grids**, each with all four purged schemes.
> - **Multiple testing**: paired-bootstrap p-values on the OOS Brier edge
>   (resampling independent 12M episodes) + Benjamini-Hochberg FDR at q=0.10.
>   A rare-event guard (≥20 positives, ≥3% event rate) blocks degenerate
>   short-horizon targets that otherwise report AUC≈0.9 from a single positive.
> - **Phase 2** `Timing Advantage = current-state expected 12M return − historical
>   mean 12M return`, with an episode-bootstrap CI, suppressed to
>   "No Reliable Timing Edge Detected" on validation failure.
> - **Phase 3** point-in-time neighbour-distance distribution; above its 95th
>   percentile a Novel Regime Warning disables all analogue outputs.
>   Analogue Reliability = High / Moderate / Low / Invalid.
> - **Phase 4** Weekly-Friday vs Month-End observations over the same calendar
>   bounds, with **time-based** warmup/windows (day-based purge/embargo inherited
>   unchanged), aligned common month-end OOS evaluation, and a conservative
>   pre-registered switch rule. Monthly stays primary unless Weekly clears every
>   criterion; nothing is replaced automatically.
>

A deployable ETF **decision-support** dashboard that reports three deliberately
separate quantities instead of one blended number:

| Measure | What it is | What it is not |
|---|---|---|
| **P(positive 6M return)** | calibrated probability, anchored to the realised long-run win rate | not a price target, not stretched to fill 0–100 |
| **Opportunity Score** | relative attractiveness, 0–100, from five point-in-time percentile families | not a probability |
| **Timing Percentile** | how unusual today's reading is vs its own 5y / 10y / full history | not evidence of an edge |

Everything historical is produced by a **purged walk-forward** engine
(`pipeline/model_v4.py`): training, validation and test windows are separated,
training labels that overlap validation are purged, an embargo equal to the
6-month horizon is enforced, and the displayed history is the concatenation of
untouched test blocks.

## The honest headline result

Testing every input on **non-overlapping monthly observations** (268 decisions,
2004–2026), **no input separates 6-month win rates at the 5% level**. The best
gaps are VIX percentile −9.0 pp (p = 0.079) and trend +4.5 pp (p = 0.38).

That is reported, not hidden. Consequences that follow from the data:

- The probability level comes from the base rate (78.4% OOS), with a modest
  40% trend tilt. It clusters near 70–85% because that is where the truth is.
- The Brier score is **0.1832** vs **0.2050** for a constant forecast of the
  training-era base rate — a real but small improvement. It is *worse* than the
  oracle full-sample base rate (0.1694), which no causal model can match.
- Isotonic, Platt and beta calibrators were all evaluated out of sample and all
  scored **worse** than the anchored identity map; the shipped model therefore
  uses no post-hoc calibrator, and the evidence is on the Calibration tab.
- Benchmarks show immediate lump sum ($10.28 terminal) beat every timing rule
  tested, including the dashboard's own matrix ($3.81). Waiting is expensive.

## Methodology guarantees

1. No future data in features, scaling, calibration, percentiles, analogues or
   thresholds — unit-tested (`test_model_v4.py`, 21 checks).
2. Appending future rows does not change any past out-of-sample value.
3. Purge: no training label overlaps validation. Embargo = horizon = 126 sessions.
4. Effective sample size is reported (`n_eff ≈ span / 126`) and confidence
   intervals use it, so overlapping returns are never counted as independent.
5. Probability calibration is reported honestly, including when it is limited.
6. Reliability bins with fewer than 8 independent episodes are merged.
7. Analogues are clustered into market episodes; one representative each.
8. A methodology audit (20 items) and a data audit are reported separately.

## Dashboard tabs

1. **Overview** — the three cards (Market outlook / Entry opportunity / Decision
   context), the 2×2 probability-opportunity matrix with one highlighted
   quadrant, the Conviction grade, a four-line history chart with 1/3/5/10y/full
   ranges, and a "where does today sit in its own history" histogram.
2. **Calibration** — Brier, log loss, ECE, reliability curve with merged bins,
   prediction distribution, out-of-sample comparison of isotonic / Platt / beta,
   stratified diagnostics (decade, regime, volatility tercile) and the
   walk-forward block ledger with purge counts.
3. **Opportunity** — family table, weights, sensitivity analysis across four
   alternative weightings, and the opportunity/timing history.
4. **Benchmarks** — ten strategies vs lump sum with terminal wealth, CAGR, vol,
   Sharpe, Sortino, max drawdown, Calmar, time in cash, worst 5%, regret.
5. **Analogues** — closest distinct historical episodes with dispersion stats.
6. **Signal tests** — every input with its non-overlapping win-rate gap, t and p.
7. **Methodology** — the 20-item audit, full disclosures, known limitations.
8. **Data & sectors** — per-symbol quality, price basis, staleness; sector ranks.

## Changelog (v3 -> v4)

- **Dividend adjustment fixed.** Yahoo closes were split-adjusted but not
  dividend-adjusted, understating every forward return by ~1.8%/yr. All series
  now use `adj_close`; the price basis is recorded per symbol.
- **Purged walk-forward replaced ad-hoc calibration.** Train/validation/test
  separation, label purging, horizon embargo, concatenated untouched test
  predictions. Unit tests confirm past predictions do not move when future data
  is appended.
- **Probability is anchored, not stretched.** The v3 "MOS" blended win rate and
  return into one number and percentile-scaled it; the blending was driven by
  opposite market states and the scaling made identical features score
  differently in different decades. v4 reports the calibrated probability in
  its natural units and moves relative attractiveness to a separate score.
- **Overfitting removed.** A 10-feature logistic on ~30 effective 6-month
  episodes scored worse than a constant forecast out of sample. The probability
  model now uses one feature plus a 7-year base-rate anchor.
- **Calibrators evaluated honestly.** Isotonic/Platt/beta fitted on a single
  validation episode produced OOS log loss 1.52 vs 0.56; a minimum-evidence rule
  (10 independent outcomes) now gates their use, and their OOS scores are shown.
- **Signal significance table added** — the dashboard now states that no input
  clears p<0.05 on non-overlapping data.
- **Benchmarks added** — ten strategies, showing lump sum beat every timing rule.
- **Analogues re-clustered into market episodes** (fixed a bug that collapsed
  all five matches into a single episode).
- **Interface rebuilt** — three-card overview, comparison chart with adaptive
  1-year axis, position histogram, methodology tab, mobile-first layout.

## Disclaimer

the Calibration and Signal tests tabs before trusting anything it shows.
